## What authority resolves

USDA's WEPP documentation defines the plant growth factor as actual uptake summed over soil layers divided by potential plant transpiration (Chapter 5, equation 5.5.1). Chapter 8, equation 8.2.15, repeats that supply/demand definition and uses the factor to limit growth. These are definitions of the original model, not validation of every subsequent forest-fork modification. [USDA Chapter 5](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/50201000/WEPP/chap5.pdf), [USDA Chapter 8](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/50201000/WEPP/chap8.pdf).

FAO-56 distinguishes potential basal transpiration from soil evaporation. Its water-stress coefficient reduces the transpiration term; under water limitation it is below one. It is not a second unconstrained potential demand. The basal coefficient refers to potential transpiration without water limitation. [FAO-56 Chapter 8](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm), [FAO-56 Chapter 7](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/x0490e0c.htm).

Taken together, these definitions support separating **potential transpiration**, **water-limited requested transpiration**, **actual root uptake**, and **intercepted-canopy evaporation**. They do not identify canopy evaporation as root uptake. They also do not specify the fork's wet-canopy energy allocation, its zero-demand growth convention, or which plant-state epoch should define the denominator when growth occurs between EVAPPM and SWU. Native SC-EVAP-001 remains proposed and does not supply those missing decisions.

## Concrete policy questions and admissible choices

1. **Which potential demand belongs in growth stress?** The documented WEPP definition supports an unconditioned plant-transpiration denominator. The current SWU ratio instead uses an already etks-limited, canopy-reduced request. Decide whether to restore that documented denominator or adopt a separately justified alternative. Using etks alone or multiplying etks by current watstr is not implied: SWU applies further layer limitations and canopy subtraction, so equivalence needs proof.
2. **How does a wet canopy change potential root transpiration?** The existing source spends canopy water against Tks. Applying the same subtraction to T0, or treating all canopy evaporation as satisfaction of growth demand, each changes the physical contract. The consulted authorities do not select either rule. This is the explicit external scientific-policy boundary requiring an approved model formulation or domain review; exact trace operands alone cannot settle it.
3. **What happens when the chosen potential root demand is exactly zero?** The supply/demand ratio is undefined then. Select and document a neutral/inactive or carried-state convention by process class. Do not silently reuse a harvest-day fallback for an actively growing, soil-limited plant. Conversely, zero root uptake with positive potential demand is not a zero-denominator case.
4. **Which state epoch and consumers are covered?** Preserve the established next-day growth ordering unless a separate decision authorizes change. Define daily and hourly callers together, and explicitly disposition EVAP versus EVAPPM. Crop/range/forest labels do not establish identical native growth paths; admission requires actual path coverage.

## Smallest defensible next correction scope

There is no scientifically established one-line correction from the currently available evidence. Setting watstr to one in the skipped canopy branch would remove this discontinuity by extending the existing neutral policy to soil-limited demand. Calling SWU at zero EP would tend to do the same and also executes its other state mutations. Suppressing tiny positive EP, adding an epsilon, retaining old stress in all canopy-covered calls, or assigning etks directly would each select another policy without resolving the demand definition.

The smallest justified implementation boundary, once questions 1–3 are decided, is **the shared demand-to-growth-stress handoff**: preserve explicit potential demand and actual root/canopy ownership, assign the next carried growth factor from that approved definition on every applicable path, and retain the existing water allocations and chronological order. Keep altered transpiration physics out of a stress-only experiment unless the chosen wet-canopy definition makes it necessary; then declare that larger scope explicitly. This is a bounded experimental design recommendation, not source-change authorization or a ready formula.

Before such an experiment, use the pending initialized operands to complete the class matrix above for both Denver directions and secondary Flagstaff. A declaration must name the chosen zero-demand and wet-canopy conventions, cover both callers and ET methods, and retain paired daily water ownership plus subsequent growth. Existing exact-allocation improvements remain necessary; they cannot compensate for an unsupported downstream stress convention. Release currently remains HOLD pending the demand-operand evidence, final repair-response comparison and independent scoped admission disposition described below; redesigning the inherited stress policy is not itself a prerequisite.

## Release boundary: existing policy gap versus repair admission

An unresolved pre-existing stress formulation does **not** automatically require redesigning physiology before releasing a separately justified water-allocation or snow-exhaustion repair. The scientific definitions cited above establish the interpretation gap; they do not require every historical model limitation to be closed in one release. Conversely, unchanged stress source is insufficient evidence that materially changed vegetation, ET or runoff is acceptable: this investigation demonstrates that different inputs can activate its discontinuity.

The current HOLD is therefore an evidence-and-disposition gate, not an indefinite requirement to replace the growth model. A bounded release under explicitly retained stress semantics can be defensible under the following review framework. The independent science reviewer must bind the response criteria to the declared repair and intended uses before candidate interpretation; the suggested variables below are not a retroactive universal measurement mandate.

- **Repair ownership:** exact allocation never spends unavailable water; complete-pack handling debits the identified current pack and credits its released water. The repair must stand independently of the stress formulation and preserve non-target behavior under its declared controls.
- **Response attribution:** pending demand witnesses classify the triggering zero/positive EP events and confirm stress/next-day growth joins in both Denver directions and secondary Flagstaff. Demonstrate that the repair does not introduce negative/nonfinite demand, a new missing transfer, a different stress algorithm, or an unintended process-order change. A response fully attributable to a retained policy remains a material response, not a waived one.
- **Final-candidate response accounting:** compare the actual repair candidate against the declared release baseline over the complete retained histories. Recommended response candidates are annual and full-period runoff and sediment, ET partition, canopy carry, available plant LAI/root depth/biomass and growth-stress observations, event maxima and dates, persistence/recovery of vegetation differences, and the propagated water ledger. The science reviewer selects and binds those needed for this repair scope. Disclose unavailable observations, including biomass if not recorded, and assess whether that limits the specific admission claim rather than automatically requiring another experiment. Where supported by the retained observations, count zero/positive call transitions and link material episodes to their demand class. Do not substitute observation-only parity or a local target improvement for this final behavioral comparison.
- **Scope and consequence review:** independently assess whether those changes are scientifically tolerable for the stated release uses under the retained model semantics. No numerical acceptance threshold has been authorized here; choose criteria before interpreting the final candidate and justify them from the use case and retained evidence. Include actual supported general-model consumers in addition to Roads witnesses. If material differences remain unexplained, create invalid states, or fail the agreed use-case criteria, the repair remains blocked for that scope.
- **Explicit residual limitation:** record that the release retains an inherited demand/stress mismatch, state its observed activation conditions and quantitative effects, and separate that limitation from the corrected water-ownership defect. Independent scientific and implementation disposition must approve the bounded scope; release notes alone cannot supply admission.

This permits a concrete outcome without invented physiology: accept the water-ownership correction with an explicitly reviewed residual stress limitation **if its actual final responses pass those gates**, or require a separately governed stress correction if the responses fail. At present, the pending operands and final repair-response comparison prevent choosing between those outcomes. The phrase “release remains HOLD” above refers to this incomplete admission evidence, not a rule that every open stress question must first be solved.
