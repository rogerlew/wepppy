# Frozen-water history findings

## Accepted experiment

All 41 declared model processes completed: 29 synthetic controls and six pairs
of full 16-year histories. The private observer preserves all seven ordinary
outputs exactly in each pair (42 paired output comparisons); original-header
baselines also reproduce their prior accepted outputs. Every history has 17,532
daily keys. All 29 synthetic stdout artifacts and 52 mapping stock reconstructions
match the retained independent controls, recovering all five known signatures.
No native equation, threshold, default or release binary was changed.

The three original KSFLAG 0 histories have zero actual frost calls. Weather
eligibility is not activation. The diagnostic KSFLAG 1 histories activate frost,
but that flag also changes conductivity: differences between header settings
cannot be attributed solely to frost or transferred to original-history losses.

## Clearing ownership and temporal representation

The first reader's physical-validity filter retained only 24 of 58 actual
clearings. Negative coarse components at some boundaries coexist with nonnegative
fine-grid totals; they must not be mislabeled as negative fine ice or erased from
assignment counts. The supplemental V3 ledger identifies every clearing from paired native branch
operands and retains separate coarse/fine component diagnostics.
Observed clear magnitudes span approximately 1.09e-11 to 0.0011304 m. Their sum
would not be a defensible annual external loss estimate.

All 58 identities reach a next observed initialization. Twenty-five also reach
a subsequent mapping first; 33 next-map endpoints are explicitly censored by
initialization occurring first. Intervening mapping and composite frost-block
snapshots are descriptive. Neither matching amounts nor a larger later fine
stock proves causal restoration of a particular cleared quantity. Internal phase
routines, GRNA and accepted external fluxes are not individually closed here.

See [clearing summary](clearing-summary.json), detailed zero/normal/high receipts,
and [readback amendment](readback-amendment.md). Original execution readbacks and
superseded supplemental attempts remain retained without alteration.

## Profile discrepancies and next target

Direction-1 profile residual ranges (fine change minus the requested coarse
change) are -0.001380 to +0.225286 mm for zero rock, -0.002063 to +0.310956 mm for
normal rock, and -0.005378 to +0.184378 mm for high rock. These are per-mapping
profile differences, not accumulated annual gains or losses. Layer differences
can be much larger because water moves between layers.

The largest positive profile discrepancy in each context occurs in a mapping
containing the top-layer zero-ST reset and zero final shortage carry. In that
branch native code does not calculate VARWAT: the reader explicitly labels its
float32 ST-minus-YST reconstruction rather than inventing an observed request.
The zero-rock maximum follows an earlier clearing of numerically corresponding
size, but intervening hydrology is not a closed budget. Final VARWAT elsewhere
also cannot be treated as unapplied water because native distribution does not
necessarily decrement it.

The next bounded native study should isolate the clearing → coarse hydrology →
zero-ST reset sequence, with matched timing of coarse and fine reference states
and actual accepted fluxes. Use multilevel fixtures and full-history regression
comparisons before proposing a correction. A global POR/UL rock multiplier,
threshold removal, clipping or invented external sink is not justified by this
checkpoint. Surface-rock observation/overlap work remains separate; profile
volume-to-mass conversion supplies none of that missing surface information.
