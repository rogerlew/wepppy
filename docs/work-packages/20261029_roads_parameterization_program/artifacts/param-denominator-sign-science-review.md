## Required sign constraint

For transport let `tT=tterm1*tterm2`; for shear let `tS=sterm1/sterm2` on the ordinary ratio branch. Both have the same transformation:

`r=1/(t-1)` and `1+r=t/(t-1)`.

For0<t<1, r<-1 and the denominator is negative. As t approaches0 from above, the denominator approaches0 **from below**. REAL subtraction/reciprocal rounding can erase the small positive t and store r=-1 exactly. Choosing+.001 then reverses the coefficient sign. Choosing-.001 preserves the preexisting denominator bound magnitude and the physically required one-sided sign.

For a nonnegative normalized slope s(x), the generated coefficient polynomial is proportional to `s(x)*(x+r)/denom`. At r=-1, choosing-.001 gives nonnegative support over0<=x<=1. In the ordinary observed first segment, the coefficients become the negatives of the invalid current coefficients, restoring positive capacity over the entire segment; the following constant-slope segment likewise has positive capacity up to the downstream boundary.

This is a repair of the sign of the **existing regularized model**. It does not restore exact unregularized continuity in the singular limit, and it does not justify changing the.001 magnitude or rewriting the ratio formulation.

A blanket zero-denominator rule needs the nonnegative-t precondition. If t<0 were a supported input, `t/(t-1)>0` near0 from below, and a positive denominator would instead be correct. REAL r=-1 alone loses this provenance. Positive first slope and positive physical shear/transport factors are expected to exclude that case, but record and document the constraint rather than assume it. A true t=0 zero-slope state is a separate degenerate limit; explicitly use the convention approached from nonnegative slopes, not a claim of exact continuity.
